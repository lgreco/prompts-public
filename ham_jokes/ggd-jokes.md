# GGD Jokes

1. At the club's Field Day setup, the Dutchman pulls up in a V12 that sounds like it's angry at the neighborhood. The Greek whistles. "Compensating for something?" The Dutchman grins. "Four cylinders is for people who apologize when they merge." The German, already halfway up the tower with a wrench, doesn't look down. "Four cylinders is for people who arrive on time."

2. The Greek is bragging about his new Cessna. "Cruising altitude, gentlemen. You should try it sometime." The German says, "I did. Once. In a Peugeot, on the autobahn, in a crosswind." The Dutchman nods solemnly. "Same principle. Different amount of paperwork when it goes wrong."

3. Net check-in on 20 meters. The Greek, in flawless French-accented French, greets a station in Quebec. The Dutchman answers him back in French. The German breaks in, in English: "Can the two of you argue about cheese on a different frequency? Some of us are trying to run a net."

4. The Dutchman is explaining his dream car — Italian, twelve cylinders, allergic to reliability. The Greek asks what he'd actually buy. "German," the Dutchman admits. "But I'd tell people it's Italian." The German, soldering a antenna connector without looking up, says, "It'll still be running after your Italian one is a sculpture in your driveway."

5. A new ham asks the Greek why he flies himself to club meetings instead of driving like everyone else. "Chicago traffic," the Greek says, "has never once given me a clean shot at a crosswind landing, so I go where it can't follow." The German mutters, "Neither can the FCC, apparently, given how late your paperwork is."

6. The German finishes rebuilding someone's rig for free, refuses coffee money, and says only, "It works. Don't thank me, just don't break it again." The Dutchman leans to the Greek. "That's the nicest thing he's said all year." The Greek nods. "In German, that was a sonnet."

7. At a swap meet, the Dutchman spots an old Rolls-Royce and admires the engine. The Greek admires the cigars in the glovebox. The German admires the price tag, silently, then says, "For that money I could buy three transceivers and a car that starts every morning." The Dutchman shrugs. "Some of us buy for the heart, Hans." "Some of us," says the German, "buy for the logbook."

8. Someone at the club asks the Greek why he's fond of German cars but flies to the Dutch Caribbean every winter. "Simple," he says. "German engineering gets me there reliably. Dutch islands make sure I never want to come back." The Dutchman raises his coffee. "Finally, a compliment about my heritage that isn't about cylinders."
