# First attempt

1. A Greek, a Dutchman, and a German walk into a hamfest. The Greek asks for the best antenna in the house. The Dutchman asks for the one with the most cylinders. The German just points at the one he built and says, "This one works."

2. A Greek, a German, and a Dutchman walk into a flight school. The Greek asks for lessons. The German asks for the maintenance logs. The Dutchman asks if it comes in a V8.

3. A Dutchman, a Greek, and a German walk into a French bistro. The Dutchman orders in French. The Greek orders in better French. The German orders in English and says, "One of you is paying, and it isn't going to be decided by accent."

4. A Dutchman, a German, and a Greek walk into a repeater club meeting. The Dutchman wants to talk about torque. The German wants to talk about SWR. The Greek wants to talk about the Dutch Caribbean, and somehow gets everyone to agree that's where the next club trip should be.

5. A German, a Greek, and a Dutchman walk into a car dealership. The German asks about reliability ratings. The Greek asks if it comes with a co-pilot seat. The Dutchman asks why none of them have twelve cylinders.

6. A German, a Dutchman, and a Greek walk into an FCC exam room. The German finishes first and checks his work twice. The Dutchman finishes first and leaves to look at a car. The Greek is still on question three, drafting a syllabus for it.

# Second attempt

7. A Dutchman, a German, and a Greek walk into a briefing before a Washington Island DXpedition. The Dutchman asks if he can just work the pileup on VHF. The German reminds him that grid EN65mj only opens up on HF, and his General ticket is barely dry. The Greek, boxing up the paper QSL cards he only prints for this trip, says, "Don't worry — by the time you find 20 meters, the ferry will already be back."

8. A Greek, a Dutchman, and a German walk into a print shop for QSL cards. The Greek orders a rare-grid design for Washington Island. The Dutchman asks what a QSL card even proves. The German, already uploading his log to LOTW from his phone, says, "It proves he flew a plane, sailed to an island, and photographed a fish before he worked you. Yours would just prove you found the on switch."

9. A German, a Dutchman, and a Greek walk into a guitar shop. The Dutchman plugs in and runs through a blues lick he's been practicing for years. The German checks the amplifier and asks about its SWR into a mismatched cabinet. The Greek, holding a vintage instrument older than the shop itself, says, "You picked up guitar long before you picked up a callsign. At this rate, you'll have your Extra somewhere around 'never.'"

10. A Greek, a Dutchman, and a German walk onto a dock on Lake Michigan for a Saturday race. The Greek asks if they're taking the boat with the biggest engine. The Dutchman, tightening the jib, points out that racing sailboats don't have engines — and he hasn't owned a boat of any kind for a good while anyway. The German, checking the wind angle against the forecast, says, "Somewhere in Bavaria there's a V12 that still misses you more than the harbor does."

11. A Dutchman, a German, and a Greek walk into a VE testing session at the North Shore Radio Club. The Dutchman is up for his Amateur Extra, hot on the heels of his Technician and General. The German, proctoring as the club's Build Club Lead, tells him the code requirement disappeared decades ago, so there's no excuse for finishing last. The Greek, who sat his first exam back in Greece long before the Dutchman's callsign existed, checks his watch and says, "At this pace you'll catch up to me sometime after I retire."

12. A German, a Greek, and a Dutchman walk onto a dive boat off Bonaire. The German checks the dive computer's descent rate before anyone gets in the water. The Greek is already assembling his underwater housing for macro shots of something smaller than his little finger. The Dutchman points out that Bonaire is, technically, Dutch territory. The German doesn't look up from the manifest and says, "Then enjoy it. It's the only place on this trip where you outrank him."

# Third attempt

13. A Greek, a German, and a Dutchman walk into the Field Day tent at 3 a.m. The Greek is running the log on pure caffeine and instinct. The German is re-terminating a connector that was already fine. The Dutchman has the rig parked on the Netherlands net instead of the contest frequency. The German doesn't look up from the connector and says, "There's a multiplier on 40 meters right now. There is no multiplier for homesickness."

14. A Dutchman, a Greek, and a German walk into a hangar at the airport where the Greek keeps his plane. The Dutchman asks if it has more than four cylinders. The Greek, doing his preflight walk-around as the flying club's president, says the engine count is the least interesting thing about it. The German, checking the fuel sump sample, says, "He built a flying club before you built your first dipole. Ask a better question."

15. A German, a Dutchman, and a Greek walk into a building-automation trade show. The Greek checks his watch and mutters that a meeting started without him back at the office. The Dutchman asks the German's HVAC controller how many cylinders it runs on. The German doesn't look up from the wiring diagram and says, "None. Six sensors, one control loop, and a better duty cycle than anything you've ever parked."

16. A Greek, a Dutchman, and a German walk into the German's shack for a club build night. The Greek admires the elevated vertical and asks if it's for sale. The Dutchman asks why there are two radios instead of one bigger one. The German, soldering without looking up, says, "One's for working the world. One's for babysitting six meters. Neither of them cares how many cylinders you have."

17. A Dutchman, a German, and a Greek walk into the club's award night. The Dutchman asks what a DXCC award even requires. The German says, "A hundred confirmed countries — about ninety-nine more than you've worked since you got your ticket." The Greek, stepping up to collect his second digital award of the night, adds, "Ask me again once you've worked your first DX station instead of just listening to the Netherlands net."

18. A German, a Greek, and a Dutchman walk into the yacht club bar after a race on Lake Michigan. The German orders a coffee and asks who actually trimmed the jib. The Greek says he just crews for fun these days. The Dutchman, who used to race sixty-footers professionally in Europe, says the boat they just sailed wouldn't qualify as a tender back then. The German shrugs. "And yet here you are, boatless, crewing on other people's dinghies."

# Fourth attempt

19. A German, a Dutchman, and a Greek walk into the club's Field Day site as the generator roars to life. The German plugs in a ferrite choke before anyone asks. The Dutchman revs the throttle just to hear it over the noise floor. The Greek, copying contest exchanges through the racket, says, "In Greece we call that QRM. Here it's apparently called horsepower."

20. A Greek, a Dutchman, and a German walk into the club's new DMR hotspot demo. The Greek asks if it can bridge to the club's main repeater. The Dutchman asks if it can bridge to the Netherlands net without him waiting his turn. The German, flashing the firmware, says, "It can bridge continents. It cannot bridge patience."

21. A Dutchman, a Greek, and a German walk into a faculty senate meeting the Greek is chairing. The Dutchman asks if tenure works like a contest multiplier — worked once, counted forever. The Greek, gaveling the room to order, says that's closer to right than this committee wants to admit. The German says the real analogy is a dupe: "worked once, should never count again, and somehow always does."

22. A Dutchman, a German, and a Greek walk onto a dive boat off Bonaire. The Dutchman says the water's clearer than Lake Michigan by a mile. The German checks his line tension and says clarity isn't the flex he thinks it is. The Greek, sealing his housing for another roll of macro shots, says, "It's not about the water. It's about who has something worth photographing in it."

23. A German, a Greek, and a Dutchman walk into the shack during a rare 6-meter opening. The German has had FT8 parked on the band all week waiting for exactly this. The Greek works the pileup calmly from his Washington Island grid. The Dutchman asks if a "band opening" is like a sale. The German doesn't look up from the waterfall and says, "Yes. Everything's discounted except patience, and you're still full price."

24. A Greek, a German, and a Dutchman walk into a French bistro. The Greek orders in fluent French just to see who slips first. The German orders in English and says the wine list is the only menu here he trusts completely. The Dutchman, ordering in better French than either of them, asks the sommelier if the pairing works with anything over four cylinders. The German says, "Only if you can taste horsepower."
